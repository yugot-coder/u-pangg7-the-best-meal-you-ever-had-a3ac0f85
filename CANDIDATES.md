# Candidate sentences — "The Best Meal You Ever Had"

Competition: u-pangg7-the-best-meal-you-ever-had-a3ac0f85
Judge criteria, in order: (1) stays on the best meal ever had; (2) concrete — named dishes, a place, a texture, a smell, one specific moment; (3) feels true to one person's memory, not a stock line; (4) one clean sentence, reads aloud well, fresh wording, no cliche; (5) lands with an image, turn or ending that stays with the reader.

## 1 — CHOSEN (168 chars)
> At two in the morning, on a wobbling plastic stool, I ate nasi goreng still hissing from the wok, egg folded through the rice, and the street smelled of kecap and rain.

Scores: (1) on subject, yes; (2) named dish + place + texture (hissing, egg folded) + smell (kecap, rain) — strongest concreteness of the set; (3) one specific late-night street moment, unmistakably personal; (4) clean single sentence, fresh wording, no cliche; (5) ending image — the street itself smelling of kecap and rain — lingers.

## 2 (197 chars)
> My grandmother's rendang simmered for six hours until the beef fell apart in coconut gravy, and I ate it with my hands on the kitchen floor while she pretended not to watch me take a third helping.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) strong — six hours, coconut gravy, hands, kitchen floor; (3) very personal; (4) good but long, slightly breathless; (5) "pretended not to watch me take a third helping" is a lovely ending. Runner-up.

## 3 (168 chars)
> The best meal I ever had was a plate of mie ayam from a cart with a squeaky wheel, eaten standing in the rain, the broth so hot it fogged my glasses and I did not care.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) strong — squeaky wheel, fogged glasses; (3) personal; (4) opening "The best meal I ever had was" is stock phrasing and weakens it; (5) good ending.

## 4 (167 chars)
> After my father's funeral, my aunt set down a bowl of soto ayam and I cried into the steam because it tasted exactly like the one he used to order for me every Sunday.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) good — soto ayam, steam, Sunday ritual; (3) intensely personal; (4) clean; (5) very strong ending. But grief-heavy: risks reading as a story about loss rather than about the meal itself, so it can lose on criterion 1/2 against a meal-first sentence.

## 5 (179 chars)
> On a night bus to Bandung I bought sate from a man grilling over charcoal at the rest stop, and the peanut sauce dripped onto my shirt while the whole bus waited for me to finish.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) strong — charcoal, peanut sauce, shirt; (3) personal; (4) clean; (5) funny ending ("whole bus waited"). Good, but the meal is slightly incidental to the bus anecdote.

## 6 (194 chars)
> I was nine, barefoot in my grandmother's kitchen, when she handed me a still-warm pisang goreng dusted with sugar, and the banana burned my tongue and I asked for another before I had swallowed.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) strong — pisang goreng, sugar, burned tongue; (3) very personal; (4) long and slightly clumsy at the end; (5) charming. Near-miss.

## 7 (198 chars)
> A plastic table in Jakarta at midnight: nasi padang, rendang, cassava leaves, one chilli I should never have touched, eaten so fast my forehead sweated and the vendor laughed and gave me extra rice.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) very concrete — named dishes, midnight, sweating forehead; (3) personal; (4) colon + list structure reads slightly like a list of courses, which the rules warn against; (5) good ending. The list feel costs it.

## 8 (181 chars)
> My wife's first attempt at cooking for me was burnt fried rice with too much salt, and I ate every grain because she had spent the whole afternoon trying, and it was perfect anyway.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) weak — the meal itself is bad by design, concreteness is about the effort, not the food; (3) very personal; (4) clean; (5) tender ending. Loses on criterion 2, the most heavily weighted after subject.

## 9 (183 chars)
> The best meal I ever had was not a restaurant but my mother's fried chicken, eaten cold from the fridge at one in the morning, standing barefoot in the dark, the skin still crackling.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) good — cold fried chicken, crackling skin, barefoot in the dark; (3) personal; (4) "was not a restaurant but" is a stock construction; (5) good image. Loses freshness points.

## 10 (180 chars)
> At a warung with no sign and four tables, the owner slid me a bowl of rawon so dark and rich it stained the spoon, and for ten minutes the whole noisy street went quiet in my head.

Scores: (1) yes; (2) strong — rawon, stained spoon, warung; (3) personal; (4) clean; (5) excellent ending ("street went quiet in my head"). Close third — slightly less sensory range than #1 (no smell/taste texture beyond "dark and rich").

## Decision
**#1 wins.** It is the only candidate that hits all five criteria at full strength: a named dish, a place, a texture, a smell and a single moment, in one clean sentence, with a fresh, lingering ending — and no stock phrasing, no grief framing, no list structure.
